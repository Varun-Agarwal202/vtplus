# Reference board

Created: 2026-10-25
Mode: Operate
Design read: A tremor-friendly self-tracking app for people recording hand movement, mode Operate, with a quiet instrument lane and clear clinical boundaries.

Sourcing: viewed via search/fetch on 2026-10-25

## Quality bar

- [Apple Charts](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/charts): charts clarify one question at a time and remain useful through spoken descriptions.
- [Apple Health trends](https://support.apple.com/guide/iphone/view-your-health-data-iphe3d379c32/ios): changing data is summarized as a trend rather than presented as a dashboard wall.
- [Kampa](https://kampa.health/): plain-language observations, honest confidence, and data ownership without overstating clinical meaning.
- [TremorTracker](https://cognitionengineering.com/app/): measurement is useful only when tied to a well-defined behavioral context.

## Borrow

- Palette/material: warm, low-glare neutral surfaces with a tightly rationed signal color.
- Type/hierarchy: direct, plain-language labels that lead with the reading or the next action.
- Layout/composition: one primary task per screen, with trend detail available on demand.
- Motion/interaction: short, purposeful press and progress feedback, never decorative movement during a recording.
- Asset language: familiar outline icons and data graphics, not medical stock photography.

## Avoid

- Cold blue hospital interfaces, alarm-like red across ordinary states, tiny chart targets, and any implication that the app diagnoses or prescribes.

## Direction contract

- Thesis: a reading should feel like a small, repeatable ritual rather than a technical test.
- First viewport: position choice, a concrete posture cue, and one unmistakable start control.
- System: warm paper ground, teal deliberate actions, rust live state, large targets, tabular data, hairline separation.
- Risk: over-polishing the visual system could reduce clarity or make a self-tracking tool feel clinical.
